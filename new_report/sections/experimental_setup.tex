\subsection{Hardware and Software Environment}
Experiments were conducted on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3050 Ti GPU (4 GB VRAM) with CUDA acceleration. The software stack comprised PyTorch 2.9.1, MNE-Python for EEG processing, SciPy for filtering, and Optuna for hyperparameter optimization, all in Python 3.12 via Conda.

\subsection{Training Configuration}
Both models used AdamW optimizer with decoupled weight decay, cross-entropy loss, early stopping (patience=15), and mini-batch processing.

\textbf{EEGNet} (14,051 params): $F1$=64, $F2$=32, $D$=2; dropout=0.516, label smoothing=0.031; LR=$1.82\times10^{-4}$, weight decay=$2.15\times10^{-5}$; batch size=32 with cosine annealing scheduler.

\textbf{ShallowConvNet} (6,003 params): temporal filter length=37, filters=60; pooling kernel/stride=100/25; dropout=0.6; LR=$3.56\times10^{-4}$, weight decay=$1.24\times10^{-2}$; batch size=32.

Both configurations emphasize regularization to mitigate overfitting, with compact architectures suitable for resource-constrained deployment.

\subsection{Performance Metrics}
Models were evaluated using: (1) \textbf{Overall accuracy} as the primary metric across all classes; (2) \textbf{Per-class accuracy} for Left, Right, and Feet to identify class-specific biases; (3) \textbf{Confusion matrix} ($3\times3$) revealing misclassification patterns between similar MI tasks; (4) \textbf{Training curves} assessing convergence and regularization effectiveness; and (5) \textbf{Overfitting gap} (training minus validation accuracy) quantifying generalization. All experiments used fixed random seeds for reproducibility, with complete optimization requiring 6--8 hours on the RTX 3050 Ti.
