This study evaluated EEGNet and ShallowConvNet for three-class motor imagery classification using limited cortical coverage (C3, Cz, C4). While both models achieved comparable validation accuracy ($\sim$56\%), they exhibited distinct trade-offs: ShallowConvNet converged rapidly with strong lateralized hand specialization but failed to distinguish central foot imagery, whereas EEGNet provided balanced classification at the cost of slower convergence and increased overfitting. Although neither met the reliability threshold for robust BCI control, their low parameter counts confirm feasibility for deployment in resource-constrained edge systems.

Future research should prioritize hybrid architectures and advanced regularization to address class imbalance and enhance generalization, particularly for foot motor imagery. Incorporating attention mechanisms, multi-scale temporal processing, or frequency-aware representations could mitigate current limitations. Additionally, extending system capabilities through subject-adaptive transfer learning and optimized electrode configurations remains essential for bridging the gap between computational efficiency and clinically meaningful BCI performance.
